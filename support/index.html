Where the money goes

OurHike doesn't take donations yet — and that is a decision, not an oversight. A funding model where money follows the trail it came from is designed and public; we won't take a dollar before the structure that honours it is built. Two commitments hold either way:

Nothing safety-relevant is ever behind a paywall.

Water, closures, hazards, and the map itself stay free for everyone, always. If paid tiers ever exist they cover convenience, never safety — staying safe on the trail must not require a subscription. That is the point of the project, not a promotion.

The trail needs money today — give it directly.

The Appalachian Trail Conservancy, clubs like the New York–New Jersey Trail Conference, and preserves like Mohonk Preserve maintain the ground this app describes, and take donations now, on their own sites — they deserve them. NY State Parks and NYS DEC maintain trails here too, funded through the state rather than a donation page. We are not affiliated with any of them — we just think your money belongs with the people holding the tools.

The model, when it arrives

The designed structure is simple to say: optional passes for the convenience of long-distance use — a thru-hike pass, regional passes, an all-access ceiling — with the proceeds directed to the organizations whose trails they came from — the ATC, the maintaining clubs, and the agencies who steward public land — and a volunteer exemption so the people doing trail work never pay for the map of the trail they maintain.

None of it is live, no dates are promised, and when it does arrive it will be on this page with the accounting alongside it. Payments will only ever happen here on the web — never inside the app — so app-store fees never take a cut of trail money.

Running costs today are small and covered by the volunteers building it: the map data is served from commodity storage, the site is static hosting, and the design goal is that it stays cheap enough to be inherited (value #7).

Other ways to help

Report what you find on the trail from inside the app — a dry spring, a blowdown, a washed-out crossing reaches a moderator who can act on it. Or help build the thing itself: the whole project is open source, and the issue tracker is where the work is.

OurHike

OurHike is an independent, open-source project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Appalachian Trail Conservancy or any trail club — it exists to serve the communities they built, and to be handed to them whole if they ever want it.
