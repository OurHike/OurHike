# v1.3.2 — Kuwohi

_39 changes since v1.3.1._

RELEASING.md §5 gives a patch the name of the minor it follows, so this one
keeps v1.3.0's Kuwohi.

**It is a patch number carrying new behaviour, and that was decided rather
than missed.** By RELEASING.md §4 the span is a minor: it adds sign-in, a new
way to file reports, an organization console and eight database migrations.
The maintainer chose v1.3.2 over v1.4.0 by poll on 2026-09-23. Nothing a hiker
already has stops working, and nothing needs re-downloading or re-installing.

What changes most on the map: every trail is drawn in one red unless you ask
for blaze colours, closures and serious warnings sit on top of everything
except ATC's own notices, the hiker's position is a new hollow mark, and
waypoints start at zoom 7 so a whole resupply stretch shows its shelters and
water. Away from the map, you can sign in, a report is always tied to a place,
and a trail organization can put in its own trails, roster and workdays.

## Named beside

**Myron Haliburton Avery (November 3, 1899 – July 26, 1952)**, who
**"was president of the Potomac Appalachian Trail Club from 1927 to 1941 and
chairman of the Appalachian Trail Conference from 1931 to his death in 1952"**
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myron_Avery)). He is this release's
figure because this is the release where an organization that maintains a trail
can put its own trails, people and workdays into the app. Avery spent his life
getting clubs to build and look after their own sections of one trail.

Wikipedia calls him **"the first 2000 Miler of the Appalachian Trail"**. It
quotes the historian Philip D'Anieri on what the work meant to him: **"two
ends connected; a trail blazed, cleared, and improved, shelters built;
measurements made; and all of the associated date [sic] published in an
appropriate format."** That last clause is the closest thing this project has
to a job description.

## What changed for a hiker

**The map**

- Every trail is drawn in one red by default. The A.T. and the Long Path are
  solid, and every other trail is a lighter dash. The legend's "Blaze colors"
  switch brings the blaze colours back
  ([#1577](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1577),
  [#1599](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1599)).
- Closures and serious warnings draw over everything else on the map except
  ATC's own notices, and a closure keeps an outline you can see at four pixels.
  You can no longer hide closures
  ([#1599](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1599),
  [#1590](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1590)).
- Your position is a new hollow mark in the map's own ink, replacing the blue
  dot. When signal drops, the last fix stays on the map and is marked as old
  ([#1580](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1580)). The mark draws over
  a serious-warning pin, so standing next to one no longer hides you
  ([#1638](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1638)).
- Waypoints now start at zoom 7, so a whole five-to-six-day resupply stretch
  shows its shelters, water, campsites and privies. The legend's Showing
  picker can turn them off with "None"
  ([#1590](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1590)).
- The ring that says how recently a water source was confirmed goes round its
  pin again, instead of cutting through it
  ([#1638](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1638)).
- New York City's pins are spaced by how crowded the ground is, and Central
  Park's paths went from 6.98 mapped miles to 29.08
  ([#1535](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1535),
  [#1532](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1532)).
- The A.T. reads "Appalachian Trail" wherever it is named
  ([#1525](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1525)).
- NYNJTC's paper map for a hike is named and linked on three screens
  ([#1582](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1582)).

**Reporting and signing in**

- You can sign in with Google, GitHub or a code emailed to you, from a button
  in the app ([#1576](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1576)).
- Every report is tied to a place, the app asks where on a screen of its own,
  and you can sign a report with your real name
  ([#1571](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1571)).
- One tap files one report
  ([#1583](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1583)).
- A note or photo you add to a report still waiting for signal is sent with
  it, even if another report is sending at the same time. Before, it could be
  dropped without warning
  ([#1638](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1638)).

**Trail organizations**

- An organization can put in its own trails, roster and workdays, and a hiker
  can suggest a club, with the club deciding
  ([#1547](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1547)). Until a maintainer
  vouches for an organization, what it enters there carries no weight on
  what a hiker is shown
  ([#1637](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1637)).

**Speed**

- The app reads the data manifest once per launch instead of seven times, and
  it is sent compressed
  ([#1615](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1615)).

## Release review

RELEASING.md §9, over `v1.3.1..a77b1ac2`, in four passes run side by side:
adversarial correctness on the safety paths, security, architecture, and test
gaps. **29 defects found. 14 fixed before this tag, 15 deferred as
`release-followup`, none a `release-blocker`.** The test-gap pass also found 9
missing tests, which are in their own issue.

Fixed before the tag:

- [#1638](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1638), closing
  [#1636](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1636). The staleness ring
  cut through the raised pin. A warning pin hid the hiker's mark. Every map
  control was rebuilt on each GPS fix. An amendment to a queued report could be
  lost. The `clubs.slug` backfill could fail the production migration, and its
  docstring named the wrong parent revision.
- [#1637](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1637), closing
  [#1635](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1635). A self-registered
  organization could give itself a maintainer's say over any water source. A
  supervisor could make themselves an admin. Nominations were an open mail
  relay with forgeable consent. A GitHub login could inject lines into
  CODEOWNERS. Deleting an account kept its GitHub login. A codeowner who had
  declined still counted toward a registry sign-off.
- On this branch: [#1639](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1639), where
  steward details stored on the phone read back without their donate and
  paper-map links. Also the stored-shapes ledger, which had run three releases
  behind.

Deferred, each to an issue labelled `release-followup`:
[#1640](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1640) (four map findings,
each of which hides or mislocates something rather than showing anything
false), [#1641](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1641) (eight backend
findings, all behind the production backend that does not exist yet, #600), and
[#1642](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1642) (three release-tooling
gaps). The missing tests are
[#1643](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1643).

Decided by the maintainer rather than found: 284 recovered NYNJTC photographs
sit in public R2 outside `nynjtc_hikes_licence`'s scope, by the maintainer's
decision of 2026-09-17 (`pipeline/R2_LAYOUT.md`). And migration `b1e4c7a9d2f6`
deletes duplicate `note_flags` rows without anyone counting them on production
first. The maintainer chose to run it anyway on 2026-09-23, since no production
backend has ever written a flag.

## What is not validated

The generator's guess was wrong: one open issue does carry `needs-field-testing`,
**[#1100 — A stationary phone still re-renders the shell at fix rate, and the
number that would stop it is nobody's measurement yet](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1100)**.

The rest of this list is what the release review (RELEASING.md §9) found
resting on nothing measured. None of it has been checked on a phone outdoors:

- **The hiker's new position mark** (`map/positionMark.ts`) is 36 px because
  that is "a fingertip's guess". What would settle it is whether it can be
  found in a quarter-second at arm's length, in sun, with a gloved thumb.
  Nobody has done that ([#105](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/105)).
  Its order against closures and warnings (the mark over warning pins, under
  closure bands) was chosen from drawn frames, not seen on a device.
- **Bottom-anchored pins** (`map/poiLayers.ts`): nobody has looked at a
  circular pin standing on its point on a real screen. It may read as
  floating rather than as marking.
- **The locate-button fix** (#1638) is reasoned from the code. The swallowed
  tap it prevents was never reproduced on a device.
- **Closure bands over the hiker's mark**: #1599 accepted this because the mark
  is hollow and a band hides only its centre. Nobody has checked that outdoors.
- **Sign-in**: whether a real Supabase token carries an `email` claim, and where
  it puts the GitHub login, come from Supabase's docs, not from the live project.
- **The organization console's limits** (three nominations a day, the
  roster-read cost, the assist budget) are the design's figures, marked
  `@unvalidated` where they live.

`grep -rn '@unvalidated'` over this span finds 63 added lines carrying the tag. The ones above are
the ones a hiker's safety or trust turns on.

## Compatibility

Nothing to do. No re-download, no re-install, no sign-in. What the phone has
stored carries forward (`storedShapes.releases.test.ts`, which now records
every release from v1.1.1 to this one). The API is backwards compatible with
all seven retained releases (`check_openapi_compat.py`), and every one of this
release's eight migrations only adds things.

No production backend is deployed yet
([#600](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/600)), so sign-in, sending a
report and the organization console all wait for it. Reports queue on the
phone until then, as they did in v1.3.1.
