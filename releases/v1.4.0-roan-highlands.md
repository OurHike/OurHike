# v1.4.0 — Roan Highlands

_27 changes since v1.3.0._

RELEASING.md §5 names this one for the balds north of Carver's Gap, next up the
trail from Kuwohi.

It is the release where the app stops being shaped like the Appalachian Trail.
The *data* went nationwide two releases ago — every registered organization's
lines, and a junction graph over all of them — but the *packaging* and the
screens stayed corridor-shaped, and the gap showed up as silences: a trail you
tapped outside the corridor told you how far it went and never how much it
climbed, a stretch you downloaded carried no terrain at all, and a coverage
cell claimed a whole one-degree square when what it actually held was a band
across one corner of it. This release closes those three, and in the same
breath New York City arrives properly — its parks department's trails, the
DOT's greenways, its restrooms and its drinking fountains, the fountains
shipping with a confidence floor that says outright that nobody knows whether
any given one works.

## Named beside

**Stanley A. Murray (1923–1990)**, chairman of the Appalachian Trail Conference and
the person most responsible for there being a Roan Highlands to name a release after.
Everything below is from the Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy — the land
trust he founded — and from the Tennessee Eastman Hiking and Canoeing Club, the two
organizations that would know.

SAHC's account of his induction into the Appalachian Trail Hall of Fame says he
**"served as Chairman of the Appalachian Trail Conference (ATC) for 14 years"**, that
**"he wrote a draft bill and lobbied Congress, playing a major role in getting the
National Trails System Act passed in 1968"**, and that he **"passed away in 1990 at
the age of 67"**
([SAHC](https://appalachian.org/stanley-a-murray-inducted-into-appalachian-trail-hall-of-fame/)).

The sentence this release is named for is the next one:

> He envisioned more than just a narrow footpath for the AT, and advocated the
> "greenway" concept to protect a wider corridor around the Trail.

That is, almost word for word, what this release does to the map. Murray's objection
was that a footpath is not the thing worth protecting — the ground it crosses is —
and the same objection applies to a dataset drawn as a buffer around one centreline.
The version of that argument he actually won is on the ground: SAHC records that
**"his work on the Tennessee Eastman Hiking Club's relocation of the AT over Roan and
the formation of the ATC's Roan Mountain Preservation Committee in 1966"** led to
**"the 1974 incorporation of SAHC with the goal of protecting thousands of acres along
the Roan Mountain Massif from development"**, and that he **"served as President of
SAHC for 11 years and was the first Executive Director."** He was, SAHC says,
**"especially concerned with how to preserve Roan Mountain and the balds in the
Highlands of Roan on the NC/TN state line, and personally focused much of his energy
on that area of the AT."**

The tidiest fact about the pairing is one nobody arranged. The shelter on the north
slope of Grassy Ridge, 3.3 miles from Carver's Gap at 5,050 feet, **"was renamed to
honor Stan Murray"** in 1995 — and what it was called before that was **the Roan
Highlands Shelter**
([TEHCC](https://tehcc.org/wiki/Shelter:Stan_Murray)).

**Two claims this section does not make.** The exact years of his chairmanship are not
written here: SAHC says fourteen years and does not date them, and the dates that
circulate elsewhere were not checked against anything the ATC or SAHC published, so
"for 14 years" is what gets quoted. And the greenway sentence is SAHC's summary of
what Murray argued, not a quotation of Murray — the corridor-protection language in
the 1968 Act is the outcome those two organizations credit him with, which is a
weaker and checkable claim, and the one made here. RELEASING.md §6 is the rule.

**Who built this one.** Most of this codebase, this release included, was written by
[Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code) working against the maintainer's review,
these notes among it — recorded here for the same reason `@unvalidated` is greppable:
a hiker weighing how much to trust a number is entitled to know how it was arrived at.

## What changed for a hiker

- **A trail you tap now says how much it climbs**, anywhere the graph reaches — not
  just on the A.T. `+4,900 ft / −4,870 ft` under the distance, and where the climb is
  only partly known it says which part, rather than quietly adding up the half it has.
- **A stretch you download now brings its terrain with it.** Contours and hillshade
  used to be the one layer a download left behind, so a stretch that worked offline
  drew flat. There is a switch, and it is on.
- **The map says when there is no map.** A downloaded square used to claim the whole
  square. It now claims only the ground it actually holds, so the "outside the
  downloaded area" banner and the dashed edge agree with the paper in front of you
  instead of appearing miles apart.
- **New York City.** NYC Parks' trails and the DOT's greenways are on the map, with
  the city's public restrooms and drinking fountains as waypoints. A park counts as a
  destination in its own right now, so a fountain in the middle of Central Park is no
  longer dropped for being too far from a path.
- **Fountains that admit they do not know.** The city's fountain data says `Active`
  for all of them, which is not a claim about whether any particular one is running —
  so they ship marked low confidence rather than presented as working water.
- **The NYNJTC hike photographs are back**, recovered from the Internet Archive after
  the club's own site stopped serving them, and a photo too small to fill its card is
  now drawn at its own size instead of stretched.
- **Reporting something is easier to do in a hurry.** The 911 line stays pinned above
  the scroll, the window itself no longer scrolls out from under you, several photos
  can go on one report, you can correct a location the phone got wrong, and you can
  just type where you were when the phone cannot say.
- **Trail names read the way the signs do.** Consecutive pieces of one trail no longer
  show up as separate rows when nothing a hiker could see has changed between them —
  the A.T. alone is drawn as 461 separate lines in the source data. Turn-by-turn cues
  are unaffected and still fire at every real fork.
- **A route somebody drew is no longer described as surveyed**, and its climb is no
  longer read off the drawing's own elevations.
- **The app opens without waiting for the map**, which mattered most on a laptop.

## What is not validated

RELEASING.md §8d: this section is never empty, and a release whose author believes it
is has not looked.

**The two standing items are still standing.** Cumulative ascent
([#91](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/91)) — the check exists and
deliberately fails for want of reference figures to compare against — and end-to-end
verification against real published artifacts
([#94](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/94)).

**One issue carries `needs-field-testing`**, and the release-notes generator asserted
there were none, so this was checked rather than copied:
[#1100 — A stationary phone still re-renders the shell at fix rate, and the number
that would stop it is nobody's measurement yet](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1100).
The number in question is a GPS noise radius, and the reason it has not been picked is
a safety argument rather than an oversight: a radius large enough to be quiet while
somebody sits still under canopy is a radius that holds their mile still for tens of
seconds after they stand up.

**The terrain this release ships does not cover the ground this release opened up, and
that is the largest honest gap in it.** Measured against the published manifests on
2026-09-15: the junction graph is cut into **505 one-degree coverage cells** and the
other organizations' trail lines into **681**, while the terrain archive is cut into
**62** — because the DEM is still built around the A.T. corridor, which is the shape
everything else just stopped being. So the terrain switch defaults on and, for a
stretch outside the corridor, has nothing to give: a hiker downloading New York City
gets its trails and no contours. Nothing lies about this — the cell simply is not
offered — but "the switch is on and there is no terrain here" is a worse first
impression than the feature deserves, and rebuilding the DEM against the wider region
set is not attempted in this release.

**Two upstream sources could not be checked** at the time of this build, per the daily
freshness job ([#478 — Upstream data
freshness](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/478)): `topo_quads`, unverifiable
since 2026-08-09, and `atc_trail_updates`, since 2026-08-13. Neither is reported as
*changed*; "could not be checked" is not "fine", and the data shipped anyway.

**Sixty new `@unvalidated` tags were added this cycle**, bringing the tree to **203**
(`grep -rn '@unvalidated'` over `client`, `pipeline` and `backend`, 2026-09-16). Two
of them are new on this release's own paths and are worth naming here rather than
leaving to a grep: `lineClimb.ts`'s `COVERAGE_TOLERANCE = 0.08`, the share of a
trail's length that may be missing before the climb figure is called partial rather
than measured; and `trailGraph.ts`'s `MAX_OFF_NETWORK_FEET = 150`, a fingertip on a
phone at planning zoom rather than a measurement. Both are picked numbers, both say so
in the file, and neither has been tried in a real hand on a real device.

**The release review found nothing, and its scope was narrower than usual.**
`/code-review` was not available in the environment this release was cut from, so
Phase 4 was a hand read of the §8b safety set — the 24 changed files touching
position, water, hazard or the map drawing, about 2,800 inserted lines — rather than
of all 277 files in the diff. 0 findings, 0 fixed, 0 deferred. A review that found
nothing over a narrowed scope is a weaker statement than a review that found nothing
over the whole one, and this is that weaker statement.

## Compatibility

**Nothing is required of a hiker.** No re-download, no re-install, no signing in
again.

A completed map download survives the update. The hash check that discards bytes the
bucket has replaced applies to a PARTIAL transfer only — a finished archive is served
from storage untouched (`client/src/lib/archiveDownload.ts`, unchanged this release).
What restarts, once, is a transfer that happens to be in flight at the moment
production republishes.

**A stretch downloaded before this release carries no terrain**, because the terrain
cell family did not exist when it was downloaded. Nothing breaks and nothing is
re-fetched behind anyone's back; downloading that stretch again is what adds contours
to it.

**This release carries a database migration.** `c9d1a7f48b62_report_photos_and_a_place_in_words`
adds `photo_count` and `place_words` to `reports` and drops or renames nothing —
expand, not contract (RELEASING.md §8c) — so the release still serving traffic during
the rollout keeps working against the new schema. It adds no table, so it makes no
row-level-security statement; `reports` is already locked.
