OurHike

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 29, 2026

OurHike is a small, volunteer-built offline map for hikers, in public beta. This page says what we collect, why, and what you can do about it. If anything is unclear, open an issue and ask.

The short version
  • You can use the map, even offline, with no account and no personal information.
  • Your location stays on your phone, except in a report you send, or sealed inside usage counts while Share usage statistics is on.
  • Usage counts carry no ID unless you choose to link them to your account, and are deleted within seven months. Turn them off in More → Safety & privacy.
  • No notifications, no ads, and nothing sold.

Information we collect

Your account, if you make one

You only need an account to submit a report or contribute. Sign-in runs through Supabase, with a 6-digit code emailed to you or with your Google or GitHub account. There is no password; Supabase sends and checks the code, and we never see it. We keep:

  • Your email address. With Google or GitHub, that is usually all the profile they share.
  • A trail name you choose and the kind of hiker you are (thru, section, day or maintainer). Other hikers see your trail name, never your real name or email.

Location

Your position is used on your device only, except in two cases: when you attach it to a report you submit, and while Share usage statistics is on, when it goes with the usage counts below, sealed so our servers cannot read it. Location is read only while the app is open on a screen that needs it, never in the background, and your track is never uploaded. Usage statistics only use a position the map already has; they never turn location on.

One tool keeps a record: Record a GPS trace, under More → Safety & privacy, notes your position several times a minute and keeps it on your phone until you delete it. It starts only when you press start, with Use my location on. It is never uploaded or attached to a report; it leaves your phone only if you save the file and send it yourself.

Notifications

OurHike sends no notifications at all — no news, reminders, marketing or alerts. (The off-trail alert this page once described was removed before anyone used it.) An automated check fails the build if the web app ever reaches for notifications. It does not cover the iOS and Android wrappers, so there this is our intent rather than a tested rule.

Reports you submit

A trail report holds what you put in it: the category, your note, the location if you add one, and when you wrote it — and a photo, if that feature arrives. Reports about other people go only to safety moderators and are never public.

Kept only on your phone

Downloaded maps, your settings and reports waiting for signal stay on your phone. We see a report only once it is sent.

Standard technical information

The services that serve OurHike (GitHub Pages, Cloudflare and similar) may log ordinary details such as IP address and browser type. We don't collect these or use them to identify you.

Usage statistics

Unless you turn it off, OurHike counts six things: first opening the app, signing in, making a hike plan, finishing one, viewing the map, and each visit, with how many screens it showed and roughly how long it lasted.

  • Each count carries the date (not the time), the kind of phone, the app version, and the club whose trail it was about, if any. It also carries a few yes-or-no answers the phone works out about itself — first count this week, this month or ever; used last month; whether an account is signed in, never which account — so we can count phones without giving any of them an ID.
  • While location is on, each count carries your position, sealed (encrypted) the moment it arrives with a key our servers do not hold. Only the grid square it falls in, roughly 8 to 11 km across, stays readable, and that is all our reports use. OurHike's maintainer keeps the key, off our servers, and every opening records who opened it and why.
  • After you sign in, we ask once whether to link your counts to your account. Yes: a code made from your account goes with each count and your account ID is sealed with the position. Our server can match that code to your account, so your counts' grid squares are tied to it while they are kept. We also keep a running total for you: when you were first and last counted and on which kinds of device; the dates of your first sign-in, plan and finished hike; how often you signed in, made and finished plans (and their miles), opened the map, visited and viewed screens; which months you used the app; and the day it last changed. It holds no position and lasts until you unlink or delete your account. No: nothing identifying your account goes with your counts beyond whether some account is signed in, and positions are still sent while location is on.
  • Counts and positions are kept for the six whole months after the month they were recorded, then deleted. Counts not linked to an account move to private files at Cloudflare about two weeks after their month ends, and are deleted on the same schedule. A database backup can hold them until the backup expires, and a position the maintainer has opened stays in a file on their own computer until they delete it. If the deletion job stops, we stop collecting within three days. Monthly totals are kept, and no figure for fewer than 25 people is ever shown.
  • Turn it off in More → Safety & privacy → Share usage statistics: collection stops and anything unsent is deleted. If you're signed in, your other phones follow the next time each opens signed in and reaches our server, sending nothing before then — unless you changed the setting on that phone or turned off account sync there.
  • Unlinking deletes the running total and removes the code and your sealed account ID, with the position sealed beside it, from counts already sent; the counts stay, like anyone's who said no. Deleting your account deletes the running total and every count still linked to it.
  • We don't record your IP address with these counts or in our server's logs for them (the hosting in front of our server may log it, as above), and no third-party analytics tool is involved.

How we use it

  • To run the app: show your position, sync your account, and send and track reports.
  • To keep the trail community trustworthy: moderators use reports to verify conditions and act on safety concerns.
  • To improve OurHike, from the usage counts, in aggregate.

We don't sell your information, and there is no advertising.

Who we share it with

  • Supabase runs sign-in and stores your account for us.
  • Trail clubs and maintainers see reports for the sections they maintain, and public reports show your trail name to anyone — never your real name or email.
  • We may disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect someone's safety on the trail.

Map tiles come from three places, and each request shows roughly where you are looking, though it carries nothing that identifies you: our own storage at Cloudflare (trail data and offline maps), OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org, the background map) and AWS Terrain Tiles (s3.amazonaws.com, hillshading and contours). Download the map for offline use and it asks nobody anything.

Your choices

  • Use the map, water, shelters and closures with no account.
  • Ask to see, correct or delete your account information by opening an issue — no personal details needed in the issue; we'll follow up.
  • Turn usage statistics off, or unlink them, in More → Safety & privacy.
  • Clear stored maps and settings any time from your device or browser.

Children

OurHike isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Security

We take reasonable steps to protect what we hold, but no storage or transmission is perfectly secure.

Changes and contact

When this policy changes we update the date at the top, and we try to call out meaningful changes on the app's GitHub page. Questions about this policy or your data: open an issue on GitHub.
