# v1.2.1 — Fontana Dam

_16 changes since v1.2.0._

v1.2.0 was the release where the app stopped being a map that shows other trails and
started being one that carries the same weight for all of them. This one adds a third
organization to that thread — USFS and NH GRANIT's White Mountains trails and
waypoints join DEC's and NYSP's — finishes the day-hike builder's map with room to
actually plan on, and gives an offline download a finer unit than "the whole corridor
or nothing." It also ships the instrument that the next real numbers behind the
wrong-way alert will come from: a GPS trace recorder built to gather the field data
[#93](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/93), [#105](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/105) and
[#106](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/106) have all been waiting on, rather than more
placeholder thresholds.

**Why a patch, and stated plainly rather than left for a reader to notice.** Measured
against §4's rule — PATCH is fixes only, no new behaviour — several of the sixteen
changes below are new behaviour: the White Mountains trails, the day-hike map rework,
stretch downloads, the trace recorder. By the letter of §4 this reads closer to MINOR.
It ships as v1.2.1 by the maintainer's deliberate call rather than by the rule, made
before this branch was cut. Nothing here strains a hiker's understanding of what a
patch number means the way a schema change or a required re-install would — every one
of these is additive or a fix to something v1.2.0 shipped — but the version number is
the maintainer's decision to own, not this document's to relitigate.

## What changed for a hiker

**More trails and waypoints**

- USFS's and NH GRANIT's White Mountains trails and waypoints are on the map —
  trailheads, campgrounds and observation sites, alongside the trails themselves.
  Dispersed camping sites are deliberately not included yet; the app does not have a
  way to say what "dispersed" means that would be more useful than an OurHike pin
  right now.
- A ninth kind of waypoint, **Trailhead**, exists — 287 of New York State Parks' own
  trailheads now show, having been fetched all along and quietly dropped for want of
  a category to put them in.

**Planning a day hike**

- The builder's map has its own room to plan in again — a side panel on a computer, a
  band on a phone — instead of a sheet that covered most of the map. Waypoint names
  and road labels show while you plan, and the route you've chosen is outlined rather
  than recoloured, so it never gets confused with a real trail blaze.
- Shelters and campsites on a planned route are stops you can tap, and a stop now
  shows what it sleeps and how far its water is, right on the row — both used to be
  stranded on a card you couldn't reach while building.
- The elevation panel tells the truth about what it has: it used to claim a network
  trail's elevation wasn't recorded, when it was.

**Downloads**

- A new option downloads just the stretch a planned hike actually covers, priced only
  for what your phone doesn't already have — rather than the whole corridor or
  nothing in between.

**Recording where you actually are**

- A GPS trace recorder, under Settings, for anyone who wants to record and keep a
  track of a walk — including through a locked screen on the iOS and Android apps.
  This is also how the app itself will get real numbers for the wrong-way alert's
  thresholds instead of the wireframe placeholders it ships with today; using it adds
  a line to the privacy policy, and it does not record unless you start it.
- Turning location off while a trace is recording now actually stops the recording,
  releases the phone's wake lock and re-enables the Stop button — all three used to
  keep running silently.

**Speed**

- The app used to freeze for ten to thirteen seconds on launch if you'd already
  installed it and had a trip in progress. That's gone — the search that used to run
  on the phone every time now mostly runs from data shipped with the map.

One new item, present-tense and hiker-visible today: **7,358 USFS trailheads still show
as "parking" pins rather than as trailheads.** [#1217](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1217) built the Trailhead category this
release ships but deliberately left that larger population re-categorised for a later,
separate decision — [#1218](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1218) — rather than folding a data-accuracy call into the PR that
happened to add the category.

## Compatibility

**Nothing is required of anyone.** No re-install, no re-download, no signing in again.
This release adds no database migration — there are no new revisions under
`backend/alembic/versions/` since v1.2.0 — so there is no rollout window to sit
through and no schema step behind it.

**One thing is worth knowing, not because it is required but because it is new.** The
GPS trace recorder ([#1181](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1181)) is opt-in — it does not run unless a hiker opens Settings
and starts it — but starting it does retain a position history on the device, which
the privacy policy now discloses in its own paragraph rather than folding into the
existing location-use language.

**The stretch-download option ([#1199](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1199)) is live rather than merely built.** Its own pull
request flagged its biggest risk as "not run: a stretch download against the real
bucket" — no cells had been published to production when it merged. This release's
data promotion cut and published those cells as part of the same cycle, so the first
real read is this release's, not a future one's.
