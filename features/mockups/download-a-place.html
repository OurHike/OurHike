Download a Place, Not a Trail

The data stopped being about the A.T. months ago. The download did not. A hiker in Harriman is still offered “the whole trail” — a button naming a trail forty miles north of them — and gets no terrain, no contours and no climb figures when they take it. This is the screen that fixes it, and the one decision inside it that is genuinely the maintainer’s.

1the tether

What is actually tethered to the A.T., and what stopped being

Three different answers, and conflating them is why this is confusing. Read down the last column: the vector data went national; the pictures did not; the numbers went national in the bucket and stop before the phone.

The one-line answer to “are we not sending the data outside the A.T.?” We publish it. We mostly do not package it. Every artifact a hiker chooses to download — the sheet, the terrain — is still cut to a buffer around one trail’s centerline, and the download screen is written in that trail’s vocabulary.

2climb

Gain and loss for every trail is mostly a delivery problem, not a data one

This is the part worth being precise about, because the expensive-sounding version of the job is already done. fetch_elevation.py computes its 3DEP tile list from the network’s own lines, so registering a steward in sources.json pulls the elevation tiles that steward’s trails need, with nobody editing a list of cells. export_network_elevation.py then measures [gain_ft, loss_ft] along every edge in the graph, and cut_trail_graph.py files it into the same squares as everything else.

Four things stand between that and a hiker reading a climb figure:

  1. The elevation leg is opt-in and defaults to off. include_elevation on publish-vector-data.yml is default: false, described as “~40 min”. Untick it and all three elevation artifacts stay at whatever the last elevation run produced — which is how the climb file came to hold 42,103 entries against a 466,966-edge graph, and why cut_trail_graph.py refused to cut a single climb cell on 2026-09-07.
  2. That ~40 min is a stale price. It was measured on run #63 (2026-08-27) against a 40,596-edge graph. The nationwide graph killed the same step twice at the 120-minute job ceiling, which is what #1287 and #1311 fixed by reading DEM blocks instead of warped bounding boxes. Nobody has re-timed it since.
  3. Nothing but a route ever shows the figure. The climb lives on a ResolvedDayHike. Tap a trail line and lineDetail.ts gives you blaze, name, length, park and closure — and no elevation, on any trail, the A.T. included.
  4. A hiker without a day hike has no reason to hold the cell. Graph cells load where somebody plans; they are not part of any download.

The honest caveat that has to ship with the number

measured The dead band is 3 m, checked on the A.T. against three published sections. Two sustained ascents land within ±4%; the rolling, descent-dominated Roan Highlands section reads +18.8% and needed a documented 25% tolerance. Harriman is rolling and descent-dominated — a junction every 1.2 trail-miles — so it matches the section where the gate had to be loosened, not the ones where it was tight.

Pulling the other way: chopping a route at every junction understates a continuous profile by a median 6.9%, p90 46.9%, and understated climb is understated Naismith time — the unsafe direction. Nobody has measured the net of the two. The maintainer’s 2026-08-25 call stands: ship the figure and call it an estimate. Every frame below prints the word.

3the unit

Three ways to ask “what do you want offline”, and only one is new

shipped · A.T. only

The whole trail

One button, 458.2 MB, the union of 62 corridor cells. Perfect for a thru-hiker and meaningless anywhere else: there is no “whole trail” in Harriman, and a park is not a mile range.

shipped · needs a plan

The stretch you’re walking

Derived from plannedHike.ts, never enumerated — which is exactly right, and is why it can’t be the only answer. It requires an A.T. hike with a start and an end before it can say anything at all.

proposed

A place

places.json already publishes named parks, towns, trailheads and long trails, with a bounding box and measured trail miles. A place is a set of cells. The naming layer #552 asked for already exists; nothing joins it to the download.

The map-you-tap-regions-on is already ruled out and is not re-opened here — the maintainer struck it on 2026-08-28: it needs the map alive inside the download window and fails one-handed in sun. The list is the control; a small map previews what is selected.

5terrain

The one question that is genuinely yours

Terrain is 80% of a place download, and it is the thing the A.T. corridor has and nowhere else does. On the published corridor sheet the DEM is 275.6 MB against the basemap’s 182.6 MB; at a place’s scale a fully-covered 1° cell costs ≈24.5 MB of terrain against ≈6.8 MB of map reasoned. Everything else in a place — lines, junctions, climb, waypoints — is single-digit megabytes.

Does a place arrive with its terrain, or is terrain a second tap?

  • Always included. One number, one tap, nothing to get wrong — and the hillshade and contours a hiker uses to work out which drainage they are in are simply there. Costs 4× the bytes.
  • A switch, defaulting on. The number moves when you touch it, so the 80% is visible and refusable. One more thing to understand on a screen whose whole point is having one decision.
  • Map only; terrain per place, later. Cheapest downloads, and the failure is silent: no contours on a ridge, discovered at the worst moment.

recommendation A switch, defaulting on — the middle one. The bytes are too large to hide and the safety value too high to default off. Precedent: the USGS raster is already a separate opt-in sheet at over a gigabyte, for exactly this reason.

7the bill

What this costs, and where it stops being cheap

Bytes in the bucket

ArtifactNationwideGrade
Per-edge climb [gain, loss] ≈5.6 MB raw for 466,966 edges reasoned 12 B/edge
Dense per-edge profile, 25 m ≈42 MB raw / ≈15 MB gzip reasoned from 3.47 MB / 694,955 samples measured at 40,596 edges
Terrain, one fully-covered 1° cell ≈24.5 MB (z11–13, 41°N) reasoned from measured 42.3 / 33.2 / 25.6 KB per tile
Terrain, all of CONUS at z0–13 ≈19.5 GB reasoned — storage is trivial on R2; building it is not

Storage is not the constraint and never was. ~20 GB of terrain cells is a rounding error on R2 and egress is free. The constraints are build minutes and a phone’s disk, in that order.

Build minutes, which is the real bill

  • Terrain is the expensive one and it is fetch-bound. The corridor’s 8,658 tiles come from the AWS terrarium bucket one at a time. CONUS at z13 is ≈559,000 tiles — 65× the corridor — which is not one job, at any timeout. So terrain is built per region, on demand, never nationwide. export_dem.py already takes --region; export_basemap.py got --regions on 2026-09-15. The machinery exists; nothing schedules it per place.
  • Climb is nationwide already and its price is unknown. 16m22s is a 40,596-edge measurement. The same step on 466,966 edges was killed twice at 120 min before #1287 made it read blocks instead of whole tiles. The first honest thing to do is tick include_elevation once and read the log — that is a dispatch, not a project.
  • The vector build already grew from ~20 to ~108 minutes in twelve days with elevation off. The job ceiling is 180 minutes and was raised as a belt, not a decision. Adding places multiplies regions, not edges — but nobody has re-measured the floor.

A phone’s disk

A place at Standard is tens to a few hundred megabytes — cheaper than today’s single 458 MB button, and additive. Three parks is not three times a corridor. The existing storage headroom work (#554, #555) applies unchanged; the place card is a better place to spend it than a whole-trail card, because the number moves with the choice.

8order

What I would build, in this order

  1. Tick include_elevation on one UA publish and read the log. Costs a dispatch. Answers whether the climb file is current, what the nationwide step now takes, and whether cut_trail_graph.py stops refusing to cut the companions. Everything below is cheaper to plan once this number exists.
  2. Put climb on the trail sheet. lineDetail.ts plus the graph cell the map already loads. No new artifact, no new download, and it is the whole of “gain and loss for every trail” as a hiker experiences it. The three states in §6 are the work.
  3. Declare a DEM_CELLS family on the client. The cells are cut and published; coverageCells.ts has three families and no fourth. This is the smallest change on the page and it gives the existing stretch download its contours back — on the A.T., today.
  4. Join places.json to the cell index. A place has a bbox; priceStretches() already prices a set of cells across families; the missing function is the one that turns a bbox into cells. Then the frames in §4 are mostly assembly.
  5. Build terrain and sheet per named place, on dispatch. --region and --regions both exist. New York City cost 1.4% of the published set and no wall clock when it was measured on 2026-09-15 — because a region is a buffer around trail lines, not a box around a city.

Two gaps found while writing this, worth an issue either way

safety-path A stretch download carries no terrain at all. The DEM cells are built by build-dem.yml and published under CELL_FAMILIES, and no client code declares them. A hiker who takes “just the stretch I’m walking” gets a sheet with no hillshade and no contours and is told nothing about it. That is on the lost-in-front-of-something-dangerous path.

doc drift OFFLINE_COVERAGE.md still says “the DEM has no cells yet”. It has had them since #1175. The paragraph is dated 2026-09-02 and is now wrong in a way that would send the next session to build something that exists.

Every Harriman and Catskills figure on this page is reasoned, not weighed. No cell has been cut over a network park and put on a scale — which is OFFLINE_COVERAGE.md’s own open question 4, still open. spike_package_overlap.py makes it a ~7-minute dispatch once there is a pair to measure, and it should run before any of these numbers reach a hiker.
