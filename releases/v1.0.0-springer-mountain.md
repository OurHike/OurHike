# v1.0.0 — Springer Mountain

_192 changes. The first release._

Everything before this was a repository. This is the first build a hiker can install,
and it exists to answer one question in a place with no signal: **where am I on the
trail, and what is near me.** The map, the whole Appalachian Trail corridor, downloads
once and then works with the radio off — which is the only version of "works" that
counts on a ridge in Maine.

Springer Mountain is the southern terminus, where every northbound thru-hike begins.
It is the obvious name for a first release and also an honest one: this is the trailhead,
not the summit. What ships here is the navigation core. Reporting a blowdown, signing in,
water you can trust because somebody walked past it last week — those are the miles ahead.

## Named beside

**Benton MacKaye**, who proposed the trail in 1921 in an article titled *An Appalachian
Trail: A Project in Regional Planning*, published in the October 1921 *Journal of the
American Institute of Architects*, volume 9, pages 325–330.[^1] The long-distance trail
later named after him shares this release's landmark: the Benton MacKaye Trail's southern
terminus is on Springer Mountain, a short distance from the Appalachian Trail's own.[^2]

The link to this release is the article itself rather than anything ceremonial. MacKaye's
proposal was a *planning* document — it argued for the trail by describing the ground it
would cross and who would maintain it, before a mile of it existed. A first release that
ships a map and the provenance of what is on it is closer to that than to a changelog.

## What changed for a hiker

Everything, because there was nothing. This is what the first build does:

- **The whole corridor, offline.** Download the Appalachian Trail's map once, on wifi,
  and it works with no signal afterwards. Three sizes, so the choice is yours rather than
  the app's: **64 MB**, **314 MB**, or **1.18 GB** for the most detail.
- **The download survives a bad connection.** It resumes where it stopped rather than
  starting the 1.18 GB again, and if the held bytes no longer match the server's it
  discards them instead of splicing a corrupt file together.
- **Where you are on the trail.** Your position along the centerline, how far to the
  next thing, and the profile of what you are about to climb. You tell it whether you
  are walking north or south when you set up a hike — it does not work that out for
  itself.
- **What is near you**: shelters, campsites, water, privies, parking, resupply, road
  crossings and viewpoints — each on a card that says where the information came from.
- **Water sources carry their distance from the trail**, from ATC's own figures, so
  "there is water at that shelter" comes with how far you walk for it.
- **Elevation you can plan against** — the real centerline profile, with the climb ahead
  called out rather than buried in a chart.
- **A closures and notices bar** carrying ATC's current warnings, with anything urgent
  raised into a banner instead of a list.
- **It installs like an app.** Add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen and
  offline, with no app store in the way.
- **Nothing safety-relevant is behind a sign-in.** Browsing the map needs no account, and
  never will.

What the build deliberately does **not** claim to do: send a report to a club (the
outbox queues them, and the backend they go to is not deployed yet), or warn you that
you are walking the wrong way. That last one is built and switched off on purpose — see
below.

## What is not validated

RELEASING.md §8d: this section is never empty, and a release whose author believes it is
has not looked. Nothing below is a reason not to ship v1.0.0; every line is a thing a
hiker is entitled to know before trusting the number on the screen.

**The two soft gates in §8, both open:**

- **Gate 13 — field-validated thresholds are not field-validated.** No threshold in this
  build has been checked against a real trail.
  ([#106](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/106))
- **Gate 14 — no real-device pass on iOS or Android.** The app has not been used outdoors
  in sunlight, one-handed, or with gloves. Sizes and contrast were chosen at a desk.
  ([#105](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/105))

**On the safety paths — lost, out of water, in front of something dangerous, unable to
get off the trail quickly:**

- **The wrong-way alert is built and deliberately not driving anything.** Its three
  thresholds — 90 ft off trail, 12 minutes, 25 minutes — come from a UI mock-up
  (WIREFRAMES.md), not from any measurement under tree canopy where GPS is worst. It is
  wired to nothing in this build rather than shipped on placeholder numbers, because a
  safety alert nobody trusts has already failed.
  ([#93](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/93), `client/src/lib/wrongWay.ts`)
- **Cumulative ascent is not verified against a reference figure.** The check exists and
  deliberately fails, because no trusted total to compare against has been established.
  Naismith's rule is used for pace and gives no credit for descent — a known weakness,
  left in place so the estimate errs slow rather than optimistic.
  ([#91](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/91))
- **Nothing has been verified end to end against real published artifacts.** The suites
  run against fixtures. ([#94](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/94))
- **Report freshness thresholds are a mock-up's round numbers.** `FRESH_MAX_DAYS = 14`
  and `AGEING_MAX_DAYS = 60` decide whether a water report reads as trustworthy, and
  their entire provenance is two tildes in WIREFRAMES.md §11. Nothing reads them in this
  build — no published POI carries a confirmation date yet — so they mislead nobody
  today. ([#256](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/256),
  `client/src/lib/staleness.ts`)
- **The low-flow water signal has not been screened for regulated gauges.** How many of
  the 199 USGS gauges sit below a dam is unmeasured. A regulated gauge lies about
  drought in the dangerous direction: West Branch Croton River reads *above normal* at
  81 cfs against a median of 14, because a reservoir is releasing.
  (`pipeline/WATER_CONDITIONS.md`)
- **Water-source touch targets have not been tried with a gloved thumb in sun.** The
  44 px chips are where the water distances are read.
  (`client/src/chrome/PoiCard.tsx`, `features/POI_SITES.md`)
- **Real OAuth sign-in has never been exercised end to end.** The auth path is tested
  against a mocked Supabase client only.
  ([#92](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/92))

**Smaller, and named so they are not discovered as surprises:**

- The corridor-zoom dot radius (1.2 px) is picked, not measured — nobody has looked at
  it on a phone. (`client/src/map/poiLayers.ts`)
- The data bucket accepts `If-Range` but does not honour it: a stale validator is
  answered `206` where RFC 9110 requires `200`. The client makes the comparison itself
  and refuses the resume, with the published SHA-256 behind that.
  ([#506](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/506),
  [#566](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/566))
- That a PWA installed on the old `ourhike.github.io` origin keeps working after this
  release moves the app to `ourhike.org` is reasoned from how service workers and
  per-origin storage behave, not measured against a real install.
  (`.github/expected-origins.yml`)
- The claim that 3DHP publishes a continuous probability-of-flow estimate could not be
  confirmed in the data and must not be quoted as a reason to migrate off the NHD FCode.
  (`pipeline/WATER_SOURCES.md`)

**Release review** (§9): not yet run for this release — findings, fixed and deferred
counts go here once it has been.

## Compatibility

**This release moves the app to a new address, and your downloaded map does not come
with it.**

The app now lives at **<https://ourhike.org/app/>**. The old address,
`ourhike.github.io/OurHike/app/`, redirects there, so existing links keep working.

What does not follow you across is the map itself. Browsers store downloaded data
separately per website address, so an archive fetched at the old address cannot be read
at the new one — a redirect moves a request, not a database. **If you have already
downloaded a map, you will need to download it again**, up to 1.18 GB on the most
detailed tier. Do it on wifi, before you need it.

If you installed the app to your home screen from the old address, install it again from
<https://ourhike.org/app/> so future updates reach you.

This is the reason v1.0.0 is a major version by this project's own definition
(RELEASING.md §4): a hiker has to do something.
