This is a beta, and worth treating as one. It has not been field-tested across the whole trail. Carry a paper map and a compass, tell someone your plan, and don't make a navigation decision you couldn't make without a phone.

It works where there is no signal

The whole point. Download the trail once, in town: from then on the topo map, the elevation profile and every waypoint live on the phone itself, and airplane mode changes nothing. Fifty miles from the nearest bar of signal is exactly where this app is meant to be.

2,197.4 mi Springer Mountain to Katahdin
~141,000 elevation samples, from USGS survey data
280 shelters, from ATC's own inventory
21,805 trail lines, from five organizations

Where the data comes from matters, so the app never flattens it: ATC facility data is labelled as theirs, water sources drawn from OpenStreetMap and the National Hydrography Dataset are marked approximate and unverified — on every single one.

What works today, and what doesn't yet

A trail app owes you the truth about itself above everything else. The honest state, kept current:

  • The full topo map offline, with the trail drawn by blaze colour
  • Your position and mile marker along the trail
  • Shelters, water, campsites and resupply — searchable with no signal
  • The elevation profile, and the climb ahead with an honest ≈time
  • Routes and multi-day plans, built and kept on your phone
  • Reporting a problem, saved on your phone, no account needed
  • Sending those reports onward — the service that receives them is not deployed yet, so they queue on your phone until it is
  • The wrong-way alert — built, but its thresholds are unvalidated, so it is deliberately switched off rather than shipped as a safety feature that might not work
  • App-store versions for iOS and Android — in progress
