2colours

The corridor, in the blaze's own colour and one grey

decided 19 Aug Two colours, and both already exist in the code — blaze.ts's BLAZE_COLORS.White = #fffdf7 and its NEUTRAL_FALLBACK = #8a8271. Nothing new is invented, and the trail keeps rendering in the paint that is actually on the tree.

The no-dash rule is scoped to above the seam

decided 19 Aug WIREFRAMES.md §3 says "No dashed trail lines (decided 2026-08-03)… Closures are the one exception." I read that as covering this map and drew the unattributed miles solid. The maintainer's call is that it does not, and the reasoning holds: below the seam the trail is representational. Nobody navigates by a 2.5 px line standing for 2,197 miles.

The original decision supports the scoping rather than resisting it. Its "Superseded" note records what actually went wrong: lines used to be dashed and "the centerline's blaze is very nearly white — so the AT read as a dotted grey-and-white thread through the contours rather than as a trail." That failure is a navigational-zoom failure — contours, a hiker following a line. At z4.9 there are no contours and no following.

So the dash comes back, below POI_PIN_MIN_ZOOM only, and grey keeps its existing meaning too — the same note says an undecoded blaze "is still the neutral grey, and the tap sheet still says so in words." Two channels for one fact, which is worth it for the one fact on this screen that is an absence. The other half of the decision is now a test, at the maintainer's request — map/style.test.ts's "a blaze never changes colour where a hiker is navigating by it (#598)". Above the seam every blaze evaluates to the same colour at z9 through z22 in all four appearances, and the blaze paint may not read zoom at all; red light stays the one sanctioned recolouring, because it is a choice a hiker arms and can disarm. Verified by mutation: making the blaze zoom-dependent turns six of those cases red, and the red-light case correctly stays green. WIREFRAMES.md §3 still needs its dated amendment in the implementation PR — a doc that disagrees with the code is worse than no doc.

One cost of spending grey this way, stated so nobody rediscovers it. Grey already means undecodable blaze above the seam. Below it, it means no club recorded. Hhighlights

Highlights are their own entity, and they cross trails

decided 19 Aug The download unit keeps the word stretch and this feature does not reuse it. What was called a stretch here is a Highlight — its own record, not a property of a trail, and free to span more than one.

The shape that follows from "may cross trails"

A highlight cannot be one (start_mile, end_mile) pair, because NEARBY_TRAILS.md settled on 2026-08-18 that "the map's subject is always the chosen trail; one trail at a time" — and switching trails "swaps the mile frame." A mile only means something relative to a trail. So the mile range moves down a level:

idstable, minted once — a highlight is referenceable across trails
nameFranconia Ridge Loop
legs[]ordered { trail_id, start_mile, end_mile } — one leg for a simple highlight, several for a loop that leaves the AT and comes back
bases[]named · published · visited — one or more, never blended
citationper basis: who says so, and when

measured The summing already exists and was built for exactly this. naismith.ts exports naismithMinutes separately from naismithTime, and its docstring names the reason: it is for "the places that must add or compare estimates BEFORE display — a route's total across legs." Rounding each leg to five minutes and then summing would let the printed total drift from the printed legs. Legs sum unrounded; the ≈ and the 5-minute step happen once, at the end.

The legs are the entity earning its keep: 8.9 mi and ≈4 h 50 m are sums across three trails, not a number anybody stored.

Two things about the numbers

unvalidated Every mile range, ascent and leg length above is illustrative. I picked them to make the layout real; nobody has measured them against the published profile. Settling them is #595's work.

unvalidated Naismith fails on terrain, in the optimistic direction. The rule gives ≈50 m for Mahoosuc Notch, a mile widely reported to take one to three hours — it is a boulder scramble, and the rule only knows distance and ascent. FEATURES.md's own line is that "a confidently wrong prediction is more dangerous than an honest unknown." Nothing in naismith.ts is at fault; nothing in it can see terrain. This wants its own issue — either a highlight carries a "the rule under-reads this one" flag, or the sheet prints distance and ascent and declines to print a time.

30the list

One highlight per maintaining club

decided 19 Aug The named list is seeded by club, not by state — about thirty entries, because that is the unit that maps to volunteering, and because the trail hopscotches between states in a way that makes a state a meaningless container for it.

It is a better answer than the one I proposed, and for a reason worth writing down: a per-state list would have put three entries in a hundred miles of Virginia border country and none in the Whites. Per-club is also self-checking — the club sections already tile the trail exactly, Springer to Katahdin, so a club with no highlight is a visible gap in a list of thirty rather than an absence nobody notices.

Two consequences to accept knowingly. The 38.5 unattributed miles belong to no club, so they seed no highlight — which is correct and should not be patched around. And a highlight with legs on more than one trail may not sit inside any one club's section, so one per club is how the list is seeded, never a constraint the record enforces.

What "locked in" reads off

lib/plannedHike.ts — "the hike a person says they are on: where they started, where they are going, and nothing else (#335)." Two numbers, stored on the phone, not synced. Its presence is the whole test; there is no new state to invent and no new preference key.

This is not the GPS-fix question. cameraMemory.ts already decided that a fix moves no camera, and nothing here re-decides it — a hiker standing on the trail with no plan set still gets the corridor view, because they are still exploring.

The one addition I made to your answer, and it is reversible. You said "probably shouldn't see this", so the mockup hides rather than removes: one Show them anyway link at the foot of the legend. Hiding a feature with no way back is a mode a hiker cannot get out of, and the link costs one line of chrome that only appears for people who have a plan.

!blocked

visited as specified cannot ship

This is the one answer I have not drawn, because building it would break a rule this repository wrote down after paying for it. I would rather say so once, clearly, than implement it quietly.

What the rule says

blocked EVENTING.md §1, rule 2, in full: "No geography, ever. No coordinates, no mile, no segment id, no POI id, no region. This is the #252 half of the pair, and on a trail app it is the more dangerous half."

The ask — two GPS points per user, no more than 0.5 miles apart, through event tracking — is coordinates, plus the per-user linkage needed to pair them. That is exactly the combination #252 removed from the report API: "a stable account UUID served next to a trail position and a time is a hiker's route down the corridor, recoverable with curl and no account." §1 goes further and names the shape: "An analytics scheme is exactly a machine for producing that pair, several times a day, for every hiker, forever."

And CORRIDOR_VIEW.md's own visited section opens with the same sentence — "Never from the eventing pipe" — so this feature's design doc and the eventing doc already agree with each other. The instruction is the outlier, not the rule.

The underlying instinct is right, and I want to be clear about that. "Somebody walked this end to end" is a far better signal than "somebody filed a report from here", which is biased toward things going wrong. The problem is the mechanism, not the goal. Three ways to get it, for you to pick:

OptionWhat shipsWhat it costs
A. On the phone, and it stays there The device already has the track. It decides "I walked this highlight" and stores that locally. The sheet says "You've walked this" — nothing uploaded, no counter, no k floor needed. It is not popularity. It is a personal record — arguably more useful to the hiker, and it answers a different question than the one visited was posed to answer.
B. Keep the published-records basis What CORRIDOR_VIEW.md already specifies: count reports, photos and thanks, which carry a mile because they must, and which a hiker chose to publish. Suppressed below k = 25. Weaker signal, and biased toward where things go wrong. Ships empty and fills in slowly.
C. Amend rule 2 Your call to make — it is your app and your policy. But it should be an explicit, dated amendment to EVENTING.md with its own issue and its own reasoning, weighed against IDENTITY_AND_PRIVACY.md. Not something that should arrive as a side effect of a mockup round, which is why I stopped here rather than drawing it.

None of this blocks the build. visited ships empty on day one under every option, so the corridor view can be built in full while this is decided. I have left the basis in the record shape and drawn no count anywhere.

What I would build, in what order

  1. Read the artifact. club_sections.json is published and no client file mentions it. A loader plus manifest wiring, no UI.
  2. Draw the corridor in two colours below POI_PIN_MIN_ZOOM — white blaze, neutral grey for the unattributed runs, boundary ticks and acronyms. This is the App.tsx conflict surface #598 warns about (12 of 27 recorded conflicts), so it goes in its own narrow commit.
  3. The club sheet and the "not recorded" sheet, on LineSheet.tsx's existing structure, with both source dates.
  4. The plan gate — read plannedHike.ts, hide the subject layers, add the "Show them anyway" link.
  5. Stop. Highlights need #595 to publish the record first, and that issue is unclaimed and free. The client half is a second branch, after.
