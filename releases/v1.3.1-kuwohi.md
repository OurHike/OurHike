# v1.3.1 — Kuwohi

_34 changes since v1.3.0._

RELEASING.md §5 gives a patch the name of the minor it follows, so this one
keeps v1.3.0's Kuwohi — the trail's highest point, restored to its Cherokee
name in 2024.

**It is the release where the app stops overstating what it knows.** v1.3.0
shipped 113 GPX tracks described as surveyed; they were drawn in a route
editor, and the climb figures taken from their elevations were understating
gain by 15% ([#1451](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1451)). It
stored photographs without recording who took them, asserted that a record was
low confidence without saying why, and let one website's timeout throw away an
entire Appalachian Trail publish
([#1462](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1462)). Most of what
follows is those four corrections and the measurements behind them.

The other half is new ground: New York City's own walking paths — NYC Parks'
trails and DOT's greenways — are in the map for the first time, along with the
restrooms and drinking fountains the city publishes, and NYNJTC's full
385-hike list replaces the 20 pages the old scraper could reach.

## Named beside

**Raymond H. Torrey (15 July 1880 – 15 July 1938)**, founding member and
Secretary of the New York–New Jersey Trail Conference
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Torrey)). He is this
release's figure because this release is largely NYNJTC's ground — their hike
list, their trails, and the city those trails run through.

The Trail Conference began in a sporting-goods store: **"On October 19, 1920
local hiking clubs gathered in the Log Cabin atop the Abercrombie & Fitch
sporting goods store in New York City,"** a meeting **"proposed by Meade C.
Dobson of the Boy Scouts of America and organized by Major William A. Welch,
general manager of the Palisades Interstate Park Commission"**
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%E2%80%93New_Jersey_Trail_Conference)).
Three years later, **"on October 7, 1923, the first section of the trail, from
Bear Mountain west through Harriman State Park to Arden, New York, was opened
by groups of enthusiastic volunteers"** — the first piece of the Appalachian
Trail anywhere, and Torrey **"helped blaze the first 6 miles"** of it.

What he is named for here is the publishing. Torrey wrote a weekly outdoor
column for the *New York Evening Post* called **"The Long Brown Path"**, and in
**1922 he "publicized a proposal by forester Benton MacKaye to build a
2,100-mile trail from Maine to Georgia"** — which is how most people first
heard of it. He co-authored the first edition of the *New York Walk Book*. And
he did not stop at the writing: **"Torrey not only wrote the columns, he
organized and coordinated the resulting volunteers and did plenty of
route-scouting and trail building himself."**

The line worth keeping is what happened after he died: **"The NY/NJTC soon
found that it took a committee to do the work he alone had carried out."**

## What changed for a hiker

**New ground**

- New York City's own walking paths are on the map for the first time — NYC
  Parks' trails and DOT's greenways — together with the restrooms and drinking
  fountains the city publishes
  ([#1436](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1436),
  [#1474](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1474)).
- Where two city agencies drew the same path twice, it is now drawn once
  ([#1455](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1455)).
- A point of interest in a city park now stays inside that park's boundary
  instead of drifting outside it
  ([#1494](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1494)).
- All 385 of NYNJTC's hike write-ups are available, against the 20 the previous
  release could reach ([#1430](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1430)).
  The hike list downloads without every hike's prose, so opening the shelf is
  smaller ([#1479](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1479)).

**Figures that were wrong, and now are not**

- **Climb on a NYNJTC hike was too low by about 15%** — a median 509 ft printed
  where 600 ft was correct. The elevations in those tracks were read as if
  recorded by an altimeter, and a 3 m filter meant to remove an altimeter's
  wobble was removing real climb instead
  ([#1451](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1451)). Under-stating
  climb is the direction that gets somebody caught by the dark, so this is the
  most important line in these notes.
- A route the publisher drew is no longer described as surveyed. It is still
  shown; it is now labelled as the publisher's own drawn line rather than a
  recorded walk ([#1456](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1456)).
- When a record is marked low confidence, the app says which reason applies
  instead of assuming there is only one
  ([#1492](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1492)).
- A trail tapped on the untethered half of the map says its terrain and its
  climb rather than showing nothing
  ([#1485](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1485)).
- The Appalachian Trail Conservancy update window says what it measures rather
  than asserting ATC posted this week
  ([#1445](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1445)).
- Trails that were showing a blank name now show their name
  ([#1464](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1464)).

**Reporting**

- A report can carry several photographs, its location can be corrected after
  the fact, and the crews who maintain the ground appear on the screen a hiker
  opens ([#1447](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1447)).
- **The 911 line is pinned above the scroll** on the report window, so it
  cannot be scrolled out of reach
  ([#1484](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1484)).
- A photograph nobody credits is now refused rather than stored, and the
  photographer is read from the write-up that carries them
  ([#1505](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1505),
  [#1503](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1503)).

**Speed**

- On a laptop the first screen no longer waits for the map to load
  ([#1449](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1449),
  [#1431](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1431)).

## Map data

This build reads the `2026-09-14` data release — **which is v1.3.0's data, and
is the one thing on this page that still has to change before the tag.**

`DATA_RELEASE` in `client/src/lib/dataRelease.ts` is the only thing that selects
a dataset. Its own docstring puts it plainly: *"A pipeline run can write
`releases/<id>/` all it likes and not one hiker sees it until this line changes
and that change is merged."* Every pipeline change listed above — the corrected
climb, New York City's trails and POIs, the 385-hike list, the park-boundary fix
— has been published to UA and to nowhere else.

Measured 2026-09-16, by fetching `releases/<id>/manifest.json` from each
environment:

| release id | UA | production |
|---|---|---|
| `2026-09-14` | 200 | 200 |
| `2026-09-15` | 200 | **404** |
| `2026-09-16` | 200 | **404** |

So production has had no publish since 2026-09-14, and the pin resolving in both
environments is exactly why nothing has gone red. **Two things must happen, in
this order, before this release is tagged:**

1. Dispatch `publish-vector-data.yml` with `data_environment: production` and
   `publish: true`. It writes `releases/<the date it runs>/`, because
   `lib/releases.next_release_id` returns `date.today()` and the workflow takes
   no id input — the id is an outcome of when the build ran, never something a
   branch can ask for.
2. Set `DATA_RELEASE` to that id **on this branch**, and confirm UA carries the
   same id. UA already holds `2026-09-16`; if the production run lands after
   midnight UTC it will mint `2026-09-17`, and UA will need a publish of its own
   before the pin can name it.

`pages.yml` and `ua.yml` each assert the pinned folder's manifest resolves
against their own base before deploying, so getting this wrong costs a red
deploy rather than a hiker's map. That is a backstop, not a plan.

**[#1516 — A tapped trail prints a silently-low climb as `measured`, because
lineClimb's `partial` guard reads a field no exporter
writes](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1516) stops this release.**
`client/src/lib/lineClimb.ts` arrived in this span (`9a1dd23c`, part of #1485)
with a guard meant to catch a trail running past the coverage cells a phone
holds. It reads `lengthMiles`, which `export_nearby_trails.py` does not write —
so the guard never fires and a partly-held trail's climb is presented as
`measured`. Understating climb understates the daylight a walk needs, which is
the same direction as the 15% error this release otherwise fixes. §8b says a
safety-critical finding is never a follow-up, so the train waits.

The rest: **[#1517 — Deduplication cannot inherit a junior's
closure](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1517)** (real, and confirmed
unable to fire with the one duplicate pair registered today) and **[#1518 —
Eleven findings from the v1.3.1 release
review](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1518)**.

**The review was one pass and its coverage is uneven**, which is worth saying
rather than letting the count above read as a clean sweep. The client safety
paths, the backend report-photo changes and the migration, and the changed
pipeline exporters were read in full; the new Wayback recovery scripts, the
`spike_*.py` files and the workflow YAML — about 3,500 new lines — were skimmed.

## What is not validated

- **303 `@unvalidated` markers** across the tree (`grep -rn '@unvalidated'`,
  measured 2026-09-16 on this branch), 56 of them in `client/src/lib` and 28 in
  `pipeline/`. Each one names a number nobody has checked and what would settle
  it. That count is the honest answer to "what does this build not know".
- **[#1100 — A stationary phone still re-renders the shell at fix rate, and the
  number that would stop it is nobody's measurement
  yet](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/1100)** carries
  `needs-field-testing` and is open. The generated draft of these notes asserted
  no issue carried that label; it was wrong, and this line is the correction.
- **Whether the release pin can be checked by a test at all** is itself
  `@unvalidated` in `dataRelease.ts`. `pipeline/tests/test_release_pin_contract.py`
  asserts the id is *shaped* like one a publish could write, never that either
  environment carries it. Only `pages.yml`'s pre-deploy guard does that, which is
  late. The table above was produced by hand for this release because nothing
  produces it automatically.
- **The climb correction is measured against drawn lines, not walked ones.**
  #1451 established that none of the 113 GPX tracks is a recording, so removing
  the 3 m threshold is right for this corpus. What nobody has is a genuinely
  recorded track to check it against — which is what would settle whether any
  threshold belongs there at all.
- **Two upstream sources could not be checked** when the freshness job last ran:
  `topo_quads` (first seen 2026-08-09) and `atc_trail_updates` (first seen
  2026-08-13), per [#478 — Upstream data
  freshness](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/478). "Could not be
  checked" is not "fine", and this release ships anyway with that said out loud.

## Compatibility

**Nothing is required of a hiker.** No re-download, no re-install, no signing in
again.

A downloaded map survives this release untouched: `pipeline/DATA_RELEASES.md`
records that *"already-downloaded data is unaffected either way: a completed
archive lives in IndexedDB on the phone and no bucket policy touches it."* That
holds when the `DATA_RELEASE` pin moves, so the step the Map data section above
asks for does not cost anybody a gigabyte at a resupply stop.

**The database schema moves, which this release's number does not normally
allow.** One Alembic revision, `c9d1a7f48b62_report_photos_and_a_place_in_words`,
adds two nullable columns to `reports` — `photo_count` and `place_words` — and
drops nothing; the `drop_column` calls exist only in `downgrade()`. That is
expand, not contract (RELEASING.md §8c), so v1.3.0's clients, which read neither
column, are unaffected, and the previous release keeps serving through the
rollout.

RELEASING.md §4 defines PATCH as *"fixes only, no new behaviour, no schema
change"*, and this release has a schema change and new behaviour both. **The
number is the maintainer's decision, taken 2026-09-15 and restated 2026-09-16**,
and it is written here so a reader can tell a decision from an oversight.

The API gained two paths and removed none —
`/reports/{report_id}/photos/{index}` and `.../link` — with no schema added or
removed either way. `backend/openapi_baselines/v1.3.1.json` is added by this
branch and v1.3.0's retention clock starts today;
`check_openapi_compat.py` reports the current document backwards compatible with
all seven retained releases.
