The seam this plugs into already exists

measured naismith.ts exports naismithMinutes() — a raw number — separately from formatNaismithMinutes(), and route.ts already sums minutes across a day before formatting once. So a coefficient multiplies minutes, and the five-minute step and the ≈ still happen exactly once, at the end. No rounding drift, and naismithTime() itself is never touched.

Two controls, not three

recommended No descent control. PERSONALIZED_PACE.md asks this outright and leaves it to you: "Most people know their flat pace and can guess their climbing penalty; almost nobody has a number for descent." A control nobody can answer honestly gets set by fiddling, and fiddling with a safety number is the thing to avoid.

It costs nothing later: descent is the layer the learned model adds, where it comes from evidence rather than a guess. Shipping two controls now does not close that door.

Why a live preview, not just sliders

The units are unintuitive — nobody has a feel for "an hour per 480 metres". The preview turns both controls into the only number that matters: what a real walk now reads. It is the same walk each time, so dragging shows change rather than noise.

Never show an adjusted time without saying it was adjusted

This is the maintainer's addition and the reason the feature is safe to ship at all. A hiker who set their pace optimistic in week one and forgot is exactly the hazard #851 — Naismith under-reads technical terrain is about: a time reading low is somebody planning to be somewhere before dark that they reach after it. Four ways it could read.

A · multiplier only
4.0 mi · 1,740 ft · ≈2h 50m
1.3× standard

Compact, and it travels anywhere. But "1.3×" is arithmetic, not a duration — it does not tell you what you would have been told.

B · was, and by how much ✦
4.0 mi · 1,740 ft · ≈2h 50m
was ≈2h 10m · 1.3× standard

Carries the baseline as a time, which is the unit the decision is actually made in, and the multiplier for people who think that way. One extra line.

C · both, side by side
≈2h 50m / ≈2h 10m std

Densest, and reads as two competing answers. On a sheet already carrying distance, ascent, basis and club, it is one number too many.

D · settings only
4.0 mi · 1,740 ft · ≈2h 50m

Rejected. This is the failure mode itself — a number that silently stopped being the standard one, on the screen where somebody decides whether they beat the dark.

Recommending B, everywhere a time appears

reasoned The baseline belongs in the same unit as the decision. Nobody plans a day in multipliers. "was ≈2h 10m" is instantly checkable against what the app used to say and against what a guidebook says; "1.3×" needs mental arithmetic at the exact moment somebody is tired.

The relationship line is omitted entirely when the pace is standard — no "1.0× standard" on a fresh install. A caveat on every line reads exactly like a caveat on none, and this one has to keep its weight for the hikers who did move the slider.

Fifty minutes of daylight, on one slider

measured That day moves from ≈5h 35m to ≈4h 45m — computed with the real rule, not illustrative. Over a five-day plan the same setting hides roughly four hours.

CLAUDE.md: "round toward caution, and say which way you rounded." A control that only ever rounds toward caution is not much of a control; one that rounds away from it without saying so is the failure this whole feature is meant to prevent.

Three ways to go

  • Allow it, marked. Same "was ≈5h 35m" line, and the figure keeps the accent so it never reads as the standard answer. Treats the hiker as an adult and stays honest. My lean.
  • Allow it, and say so once. As above, plus a one-time note when they cross below standard. Costs a friction point on a setting most fast hikers legitimately want.
  • Clamp at standard. Slowest-only. Safest and slightly dishonest — a genuinely fast hiker is then told a number the app knows is wrong for them, which is its own way of not being trusted.

Two call sites must not get the coefficient

naismith.ts feeds eleven places. Threading a multiplier through all of them looks like the careful thing to do and would be wrong twice.

Call siteCoefficientWhy
highlightDetail.tsyesThe sheet that started this. Carries the relationship line.
route.tsyesSums raw minutes then formats once — the cleanest place it lands.
RouteStopsPanel.tsxyesRenders route.ts's minutes; inherits it.
ElevationRibbon.tsxyesSame day, same number — must agree with the panel above it.
spurDestination.tsyes"≈15m there and back" is a walking time like any other.
Plan.tsxyesDisplays the route's totals.
dayReach.tsNO Already personal. Its own docstring: it reads the miles a hiker's own days covered, off their trip log, including the two hours at the shelter. Multiplying it applies the personalization twice — and it is the instrument that answers "which piece fits the five days I have."
upcomingClimb.tscare MIN_CLIMB_FT is derived as the smallest climb that can move the number the callout prints. Scale the number and that derivation changes with it. Either re-derive against the hiker's coefficient or state plainly that the threshold stays on the standard rule.

And naismithTime() itself is not touched

PERSONALIZED_PACE.md is firm and correct here: naismith.ts has no descentFt parameter "not just 'ignored if passed' but structurally absent, so a future call site can't accidentally wire descent in without touching this function's signature first." The personalized estimator is a new function alongside it. The guard did its job; the replacement keeps the property.

What I would build, in what order

  1. The coefficient and its storage. A new estimator beside naismithMinutes(), the preference on the path units already uses, defaulting to standard so nothing changes until somebody moves a slider.
  2. The relationship line, treatment B, and the rule that it is omitted at standard. Built before the control, so no screen can ever show an adjusted time without it.
  3. The Pace screen with two controls and the live preview.
  4. Thread the six call sites, leave dayReach.ts alone, and decide upcomingClimb.ts's threshold explicitly rather than by omission.
  5. A test that no surface prints an adjusted time without its baseline — the invariant, held the way style.test.ts holds the blaze colour.

Two things I need from you before step 3

  • Faster than standard — allow marked, allow with a one-time note, or clamp.
  • Descent control — I recommend leaving it out and letting the learned layer earn it. Say if you want all three.
