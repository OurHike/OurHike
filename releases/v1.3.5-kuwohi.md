# v1.3.5 — Kuwohi

_78 changes since v1.3.4._

RELEASING.md §5 gives a patch the name of the minor it follows, so this one
keeps v1.3.0's Kuwohi.

**It is a patch number carrying new behaviour, and that was decided rather
than missed.** By RELEASING.md §4 the span is a minor: it adds weather on the
waypoint card, podcasts, challenges, seventeen more trail organizations and
three database migrations. The maintainer chose v1.3.5 over v1.4.0 by poll on
2026-10-20, as v1.3.2 was chosen over v1.4.0 on 2026-09-23. Nothing a hiker
already has stops working, and nothing needs re-downloading or re-installing.

What changes most: a tapped waypoint now shows NOAA's forecast for that spot,
closed trails and trailheads are crossed out, water distances say where they
came from and mark an estimate, and seventeen more trail organizations' trails
reach the map. Underneath, every file a phone downloads is now built by a new
data pipeline (#1805), and this release is the first to ship it.

## Named beside

**Earl Shaffer**, who is **"best known as the first person to hike the entire
length of the Appalachian Trail in one, continuous journey"**. He **"set out
do so on April 4, 1948"** from Mt. Oglethorpe in Georgia and **"reached Mt.
Katahdin in Maine, in 124 days, averaging 17 miles per day"**. In 1965 he
**"hiked in 99 days from Maine to Springer Mountain"**, and in 1998, **"at age
79"**, he walked it northbound again
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Shaffer), read 2026-10-20).

He is this release's figure because of what he wrote down. Wikipedia says he
**"carefully documented his northbound hike through photography, entries into
notebooks kept at trail shelters"** and **"the trail journal he called his
'Little Black Book'"**, and that **"Earl's 1948 trail journal has been both
scanned and transcribed by volunteers"**. This release is about the same
thing: saying where each piece of trail information came from, and when.

## What changed for a hiker

**On a waypoint**

- A tapped waypoint shows NOAA's National Blend of Models forecast for that
  spot: one line when you first tap it, and a "Weather here" band when you
  open the card ([#1782](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1782),
  [#1722](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1722)).
- A tapped shelter or campsite names its parts, such as its privy and water,
  as chips under its name
  ([#1713](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1713)).
- Water distances say where they came from, and a distance that is an
  estimate is marked as one
  ([#1788](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1788)).

**Closures**

- A closed trail is crossed out instead of banded in red
  ([#1693](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1693)).
- A trailhead is crossed out when every trail within 100 m of it is closed
  ([#1705](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1705)).
- Closures, hazard areas and notices from more trail organizations reach the
  map through the new data pipeline
  ([#1805](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1805)).

**The map**

- Waypoint pins were redrawn: smaller inside their tap area, pale below
  trailheads, and hollow when nobody has confirmed them. A crowded pin falls
  back to a dot instead of disappearing
  ([#1684](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1684),
  [#1681](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1681),
  [#1691](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1691)).
- Stream crossings are gone from the map and the legend
  ([#1680](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1680)).
- Only hiking trails are drawn. New Hampshire's snowmobile corridors and the
  Forest Service's motorized trails are no longer shown as hiking trails
  ([#1678](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1678),
  [#1712](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1712)).
- Seventeen more trail organizations' trails reach the map, and a trail
  carries its steward's own trail marker on its badge
  ([#1786](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1786),
  [#1779](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1779),
  [#1544](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1544)).
- With "Blaze colors" on, trails keep their dashes instead of all turning
  grey ([#1700](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1700)).
- The trails-in-view list and the "drawn waypoints" count no longer get
  stuck when a map tile hangs
  ([#1699](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1699),
  [#1694](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1694)).
- Faint text is darker, so it is easier to read
  ([#1679](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1679)).

**New**

- Challenges: a trail club's list of places on its own trails, which you can
  join and tick off at camp, starting with the ATC's Summer Bucket List
  ([#1798](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1798),
  [#1807](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1807)).
- Podcast episodes picked for a hike or tagged to a place, opened in your own
  podcast app ([#1686](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1686)).

**Downloaded maps**

- A route graph you have downloaded keeps working with one bar of signal,
  after a data update, and when the data it came from is gone
  ([#1836](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1836),
  [#1829](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1829)).
- Fixed before it shipped: a phone holding a downloaded map could open with
  an empty Map tab until the app was restarted. The defect was introduced
  after v1.3.4, so no released build had it
  ([#1866](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/pull/1866)).

## Map data

This build's compiled floor is the `2026-10-10-2` data release, and
production's pointer (`channels.json`) names `2026-10-17-2`, so a phone with
signal reads `2026-10-17-2`. v1.3.4 read `2026-09-24-2`.

**This is the first release since production's data moved by promotion**
(RELEASING.md §10, amended 2026-10-16). Both releases were copied from UA by
`promote-data.yml` on 2026-10-20
([run 1](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/actions/runs/39436378047),
[run 2](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/actions/runs/39443129726)), each
object checked against its UA source by size and MD5.

**Production served no release folder at all between the bucket move and that
promotion.** The bucket `our-hike` replaced `your-hike` on 2026-10-02, and its
contents were not copied (LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md 1.1). UA was refilled by its own
publishes, and production never was. Measured on 2026-10-20 before the
promotion: `releases/2026-09-24-2/manifest.json`, v1.3.4's floor, answered 404
at `data.ourhike.org`, so a fresh install of v1.3.4 in that window most likely
got no map data. Phones that had already downloaded data kept it.

**Gate 6 is not green, and this release ships anyway by the maintainer's
decision (poll, 2026-10-20).** `verify-release.yml` reads the bucket's root
`latest.json`, which in production now lists only the hourly conditions files,
so it cannot reach a promoted release
([#1873 — verify-release.yml cannot verify a promoted release, and three of its

## Compatibility

Nothing to do. No re-download, no re-install, no sign-in. What the phone has
stored carries forward (`storedShapes.releases.test.ts` now records every
release from v1.1.1 to this one, v1.3.5's entry included). The API is
backwards compatible with every retained release (`check_openapi_compat.py`),
and all three of this release's migrations only add tables, columns and
indexes. They were applied to UA and then production on 2026-10-20
([Migrate run 41](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/actions/runs/39432896289)).

No production backend is deployed yet
([#600](https://github.com/OurHike/OurHike/issues/600)), so sign-in, sending a
report and the organization console all wait for it. Challenges work on the
phone, and a completed item's tag queues there until then, as reports do.
