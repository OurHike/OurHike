            FOR THE PEOPLE WHO CUT THE TREAD
          [h1] Your trails, drawn from your own data.
            Nobody knows your sections like you do — so nobody else should be the one to publish them. Hand us your GIS and your hikes, and we will put them on every phone that walks them, offline, for free.
            [button] Add your trails
            [button] See the demo org
            [svg]
                OurHike is a beta, and you should treat it as one.
              Your data stays yours, every section you publish is dated and reversible, and if this project ever goes away the map and the tooling are open source — you fork it and keep going. That is the promise Avenza's shutdown taught us to make in writing.

        [h2] Four reasons orgs register
        Everything below is true on the day you finish. None of it asks you to change how you already work.
          [svg]
          [h3] Beautiful maps, free to every hiker
            Your sections drawn from your own geometry, with your own blaze colours, working with no bars and no data plan.
          [svg]
          [h3] Crowd-source trail problems
            A blowdown reported at noon reaches the maintainer who covers that mile — not a generic inbox.
          [svg]
          [h3] Every hiker learns about volunteering
            Your workdays appear where hikers already look — engage more volunteers, more frequently.
          [svg]
          [h3] Members and donations reach you directly
            The money goes to trail organizations, not a VC-backed tech company. OurHike is maintained by volunteers and keeps nothing beyond what running it costs — we publish the whole split, payment fees included, before we ever ask you to opt in.
            [svg]
              DEMO ORG · FAKE DATA, REAL GROUND
              Central Park Throughikers
              Want to see what your own pages would look like before you type anything? We keep a demo org on Central Park — six routes, 13.8 miles, 29 invented volunteers. Poke at all of it; nothing you do there matters.

        [h2] What registering actually involves
        Registering takes five minutes today. Three stages follow it, and any of your admins can pick up any of them — nothing expires. After sign-off your trails are live, and the volunteer work carries on from there.
        [svg]
              [svg]
              THE ON-RAMP · TODAY
            [h3] Register the org
              Your web address, three admins, and where your giving pages live. About five minutes.
              [svg]
              STAGE 1 · A DAY OR TWO
            [h3] Admins approve
              Each of the three says yes by email. Whoever answers first picks up the registry.
              [svg]
              STAGE 2 · THE LONG CLIMB
            [h3] The hike registry
              Your GIS sections and featured hikes, read with you. An afternoon if your files are tidy.
              [svg]
              STAGE 3 · SIGN-OFF
            [h3] All three confirm it
              Your admins read the registry line by line. When they agree it publishes — and onboarding is done.
              [svg]
              THEN · ONGOING
            [h3] Your volunteers
              Roles, coverage, your roster and their welcome emails. Not a stage to finish — the work the tool exists for.

              Where the money goes
              Today this is a traffic job, not a treasury one: give us your own membership and donation pages and we put them in front of every hiker walking your sections. Hikers pay you directly, and we never see it.
                    Membership page
                  [input text] https://ramapotrails.org/join value=
                    Donation page
                  [input text] https://ramapotrails.org/give value=
              [svg]
                    If we ever collect on your behalf, here is who takes what.
                  Money would pass through Open Source Collective as our nonprofit fiscal host, which charges 10%. Stripe takes its published card fee. OurHike keeps a small share for servers and map hosting — no profit, no investors. Everything left is yours. Longer term we may form our own non-profit, which would cut that 10% host fee down as far as we can get it — more of the money would reach you, never us. We are also weighing holding back 1–3% as a long-term fund for the tool itself, and for grants to the things that make a trail worth walking twice: a good bench, a summit register, a hand-carved sign. Help everyone hike their own hike.
                    None of this is live yet, and none of it is promised. The shape being discussed is a quarterly payout once a club clears $1,000, net of those three shares. Want the exact rates emailed to your admins before anything starts?
