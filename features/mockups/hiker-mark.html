The hiker is the one thing on this map that is not a place

The map draws where a hiker stands with MapLibre's stock blue dot: 15 px, a hue one degree off the water pin's, pulsing every two seconds, fed by a GPS watch of its own that the header never sees. Four ways to draw it as what it is — a viewer among places — each drawn at 1:1 against the pins it has to be found among, on the shelter site a hiker walks past and on the densest network we ship.

Decided the same day, and built into the pull request this page rides

The maintainer picked option A, the reticle (#1581 — Draw the hiker as a hollow reticle in the sheet's own ink, fed by the header's GPS watch, and retire the stock blue dot). What shipped with it: map/positionMark.ts rasterises the mark in three ink families, live and stale; map/positionLayers.ts draws it above every other layer with the accuracy ring under it; map/mapChrome.ts's LocateControl replaces GeolocateControl, and the second GPS watch goes with it; lib/useGeolocation.ts carries the last fix through unavailable, so the stale state below is reachable. The dash was borrowed: on this map it now means nobody has verified this on a pin and nobody has heard from the GPS since on the mark. B and C stay drawn and unbuilt; D was not needed. {grade('decided', 'decided · 2026-09-17')}

The hard case: 46 waypoints on five blazed trails, 19 of them winning a pin under the same greedy placement MapLibre runs, the rest dots. Two water pins sit within 60 px of the hiker in the hiker's own hue. The pulse is the only thing that finds it, and the design system forbids the pulse.

Six things the stock dot gets wrong, and what each rests on

  1. It is water-blue. #1da1f2 is hue 202.8°; the water pin's #1c6ea4 is 203.8°. poiIcons.ts spent a paragraph finding the ninth accent 24° clear of its neighbour, and the mark for the hiker sits one degree inside the first. measured · hue of the two hexes
  2. It is pin-sized in the wrong direction. 19 px with its ring: above the dot rank's 2.5–4 px, half the pins' 30.4–38. In grey it is a small pin, and a pin is a claim that something is here. reasoned
  3. It moves, and nothing else does. .maplibregl-user-location-dot:before scales to 3× and fades every 2 s, forever. WIREFRAMES.md's motion rule is "120–200 ms ease fades only … No bounce, no spring." The dot is the one element on the trail screen outside it. measured · the keyframes in maplibre-gl.css
  4. It has its own GPS watch. GeolocateControl runs watchPosition internally; lib/useGeolocation.ts runs the header's. mapChrome.ts calls it "a SECOND high-accuracy watch on one battery", and backgroundGeolocation.ts counts three with the trace recorder on. #312 — GPS permission is a one-shot: skipping onboarding disables it forever, the header never stops "Looking for GPS…", and the locate control bypasses the gate entirely gated the control and left the watch. measured
  5. It can disagree with the header. On a lost fix the control greys its dot in place (-stale, #aaa) while positionLine.ts prints No GPS signal — and useGeolocation's unavailable state carries no position at all, so the header has nothing to agree with. Two answers on one screen about the one thing it exists to answer. reasoned · two state machines, nobody has caught it
  6. Its accuracy has no floor and its contrast no margin. The 95 % radius at 20 % blue: a 10 m fix at z14 is 2.7 px, hidden under the dot; a 100 m canopy fix is a 27 px wash with no edge. The dot sits at 2.83:1 on the sheet's white paper and 2.27:1 on woodland. And the app's own accuracyFeet is "captured in useGeolocation and consumed by nothing" — #315 — Safety-review follow-ups: smaller items, none worth its own issue's finding, still true (grep: the hook alone). measured

02the brief

What a mark for the hiker has to do

Eight tests, each with the place it comes from. An option that fails one is not thereby out; an option that fails one silently is.

  1. Found in a quarter-second among 38 px accent discs — by shape, because the pins' own first rule is that hue is the second channel and shape the first (poiIcons.ts, decision 1; WIREFRAMES.md 9d). measured · the rule the pins ship under
  2. Never read as a place. Pins and dots already own "something is here", and own it completely: "a pin or a dot and never as neither" (features/POI_VISIBILITY.md). A third filled disc joins that sentence whether it wants to or not. reasoned
  3. Never read as an alert. Red belongs to closure tape, ATC notices and the serious-warning pin; the triangle belongs to the warning and the tent. poiIcons.ts: "a spring that reads at a glance as do not walk down there is a worse failure than an ugly palette." measured · poiIcons.test.ts holds it
  4. Says how sure it is. Draw the 95 % radius where it is bigger than the mark and nothing where it is not — a confident point under canopy is the wrong-way alert's false positive arriving through a different door. reasoned · CLAUDE.md, an honest unknown outranks a confident answer
  5. Says how old it is, in the header's own words. A lost fix looks lost, and the mark and the mono line agree — which means one watch, not two. reasoned
  6. Works at every zoom. At z4.87 the opening camera draws lines only (#1292 — Below the seam the map draws marks that read as waypoints; the opening camera should draw trail lines only), so the mark is then the only point on 2,197 miles; at z16 it sits inside a pin. measured
  7. Never culled. Placement drops symbols that collide (drawnPois.ts); the mark has to ignore placement and sit above the pins it would otherwise lose to. A DOM marker sits above everything, labels and the card's tether included, which is the other wrong answer. measured · queryRenderedFeatures documents the drop
  8. Costs the night nothing. The night_hike sheet's brief is "as little light as possible" (liveTopo.ts); under red light every hue is #e8804a; in sun, greyscale. A mark that is mostly hole spends almost no light. reasoned

A ninth, arriving today. #1575 — Draw every trail as one red line unless the hiker switches "Blaze colors" on in the legend would make red the ground every line is drawn on by default. Whichever way that lands, it is the strongest argument for a mark that never borrowed a hue: the states strip under option A draws both.

03option A

Option A — The reticle

A hollow ring, a centre dot, four ticks. Drawn in the sheet's own ink — stone-900 on paper, bone-100 on the night sheet, the red-light amber under red light — and in no hue of its own. Every place on this map is a filled disc; the hiker is the one hollow thing. It is also the locate button's own glyph at map scale: MapLibre's icon is a ring, a dot and four ticks (read off the data URI in maplibre-gl.css), so the thing a hiker taps and the thing that appears are the same shape.

Sizes, and what they rest on

Ring radius 11 px at 2.5 px of ink with 1.5 px of paper either side; ticks from 13 to 17 px; a 2.5 px dot. A 36 px box, 2 px under a pin, and 224 px² of ink against a pin disc's 1,134 (20 %). measured · arithmetic on the geometry above

Inside a pin rather than around one, so standing at a shelter leaves the shelter readable through the ring. reasoned The floor itself is a fingertip's guess: what would settle it is whether 36 px is found in a quarter-second at arm's length in sun with a gloved thumb, which is #105 — Outdoor usability pass — sunlight glare and gloved, one-handed use's walk and nobody else's. @unvalidated

Ink 15:1 on the sheet's paper, 12:1 on woodland; bone 15:1 on the night sheet's ink. The stock dot's 2.8:1 and 2.3:1 are in the first callout. measured

The dash is borrowed knowingly. On a pin, RIM_DASHES's eight dashes mean nobody has verified this exists. On the mark they would mean nobody has heard from the GPS since. The same family of doubt, a different object — and one dash meaning two doubts is a thing to decide, not to inherit. The alternative is a fade with the age and no dash. decide

The other hollow ring on this map is the dispute footnote (disputeMark.ts): 18 px, "a ring with a bar through it", drawn beside a pin. The reticle is 36 px, barred by nothing, and never beside anything. Whether the two are confusable at arm's length nobody has looked. @unvalidated

When the tick is drawn

Exactly when positionLine prints a mile, and never otherwise: not for Off the trail, not for No trail taken, not for No miles on the L.P., and under a followed day hike only at the route's own nearest point — the projection OffRouteCard already reports in words. reasoned · one decision, already made in positionLine.ts

No leash. Nothing is drawn between the two marks. dayHikeFollow.ts's refusal stands: "a routed line across that ground would be the app drawing a path where none exists." The gap is the information. decided · lib/dayHikeFollow.ts

The risk is two marks reading as two answers. Nobody has put two marks in front of a hiker at a junction. What would settle it is the same field walk as everything else on this page. @unvalidated

Composes with A rather than replacing it: the reticle is the phone's truth, the tick is the app's claim, and CLAUDE.md's "never let a display outrun its source" is the reason to draw them as two things.

05option C

Option C — The headlamp

The reticle with a beam: a 60° wedge, 32 px long, at 16 % ink, along the direction of travel — drawn while a movement bearing exists and gone the moment it does not. Standing still has no beam, which is true of a headlamp too. It carries on the canvas what the header carries in a word (NOBO), and at a junction it is the one thing a hiker actually wants to know.

Blocked, and on what

No bearing exists. features/HIKER_SAFETY.md §5: the wrong-direction mode "was never built at all … no such computation ever existed in the client." #93 — Wrong-way alert thresholds are wireframe placeholders, not validated numbers removed the feature that would have carried it. blocked

Which bearing. features/UX_CUSTOMIZATION.md: prefer a GPS-derived movement bearing while walking; the device compass is noisy near poles and stoves, and on iOS under Capacitor needs a WKUIDelegate callback in the shell before the permission prompt even appears. So the beam is a trailing-window trajectory, which is exactly the maths #93's removal took with it.

Miss rather than cry wolf. A beam pointing the wrong way at a junction is a confident wrong answer about the one thing this map must not guess. It wants the follow state's hysteresis (dayHikeFollow.ts) before it draws, and to vanish rather than dither. reasoned

Until then it is option A. Nothing in A has to change to gain the beam later: the wedge is a second image under the same symbol.

07set aside

Considered, drawn, and set aside

Three marks that were drawn and set down

  • A walking figure. Limbs at a 36 px box are 3 px wide, and the trailhead glyph's note in poiIcons.ts already records what happens to small detail at pin size: the boot prints "silt up into one blob." reasoned
  • The white blaze itself — a 2×6 rectangle, the most A.T. shape there is. White on a white-blazed line vanishes in greyscale on the one trail that matters most, and the through-route badge already wears a blaze chip (trailBadges.ts). reasoned
  • The accuracy wash as the mark. A disc with no edge is a wash, and a wash under a pin is the pin's shadow. reasoned

And no hue of its own, because there is none left

Every accent on the wheel, with the bars poiIcons.ts holds them to (30° off the workday pin, 15° off the closure red, AA on the halo). 09building A

What building A needs, and what is still the maintainer's to decide

  • map/positionLayers.ts (new): a circle layer for the accuracy ring, radius max(floor, r95 px) from the fix's accuracy through an exponential-by-zoom expression, and a symbol layer for the reticle with icon-allow-overlap and icon-ignore-placement, added last so it sits above pins, labels and tape. A circle takes no part in placement (poiLayers.ts's dot rank is the precedent); a symbol that ignores it evicts nothing.
  • map/positionMark.ts (new): the ring rasterised at 2× through poiIcons.ts's primitives, four inks (day, night, red light, stale), the dashed rim through the same rimHasInk the pins use.
  • MapView.tsx: a fix prop carrying GeolocationState, so the canvas reads the watch the header reads. Today the canvas receives only locationEnabled.
  • mapChrome.ts: GeolocateControl replaced by a LocateControl built the way ReportControl is — one button, MapLibre's own icon — calling App's existing handleBackToMe. The second watch leaves with it; LOCATE_MAX_ZOOM keeps its job, and #315's "should lock be the resting state" is answered by the mapTaken latch that #910 — Planning a trip on a phone shows no terrain at all, because the ribbon only exists where there is a GPS fix's review already built.
  • lib/useGeolocation.ts: carry the last fix and its fixedAt through unavailable, so the mark can be stale rather than gone. decide
  • A recipe, client/preview-shots/trail-screen-located.mjs, with Playwright's context.setGeolocation at a fixed, published on-trail coordinate — never a real fix (features/FLOW_TESTING.md; .claude/skills/pr-screenshot).
  • Tests: the layer is last; it survives a viewport packed with pins; the floor holds at z9 and z16; the rim dashes on unavailable and draws nothing on denied, idle and unsupported; the mark and positionLine never disagree on the same state.

Five things only the maintainer can decide

  1. Whether the pins' dash may also mean "stale", or the lost mark fades instead.
  2. The floor: 11 px inside a pin, or a ring that encloses one.
  3. Whether the accuracy ring draws below z12 at all, or only at walking zooms.
  4. Whether B's tick is wanted at all, given the two-marks risk.
  5. Whether the locate button survives as a custom control, or the mark is enough and the button goes.

Drawn by a generator rather than by hand, so every pin here is the geometry map/poiIcons.ts ships: the polygons are the same glyph rings, the badge sits at 0.555 of the rim, the unverified rim has eight dashes. The dense frame's placement is a greedy pass in POI_PRIORITY order with 42 px boxes, the simulation pipeline/spike_poi_seam.py runs, before #1536's crowding padding, which would draw fewer pins still. Metres per pixel are at 40°N with 512 px tiles. Nothing on this page was rendered by MapLibre, and the real map differs from a drawing in the ways a drawing always does; the ground is synthetic and no frame is a real place.
