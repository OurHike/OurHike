The derived numbers, worked

reasoned ≈2 h for McAfee Knob: 3.5 mi × 1.609344 = 5.633 km, ÷ 5 km/h = 67.6 min; 1,740 ft × 0.3048 = 530.4 m, ÷ 600 × 60 = 53.0 min; 120.6 min → 5-minute step → 120 min. The arithmetic is naismith.ts's, unchanged.

unvalidated Mahoosuc Notch is on that screen on purpose, and its number is wrong. Naismith gives ≈50 m for a mile that is widely reported to take hikers one to three hours — it is a boulder scramble, and the rule only knows distance and ascent. The estimate is wrong in the optimistic direction, which is the one FEATURES.md calls out: "a confidently wrong prediction is more dangerous than an honest unknown." Nothing in naismith.ts is at fault; nothing in it can see terrain. I would raise this as its own issue rather than fix it here — either a stretch carries an optional "the rule under-reads this one" flag, or the corridor view shows distance and ascent and declines to print a time. Your call which, but it should not ship without one.

unvalidated Every mile range and ascent on these three screens is illustrative. I picked them to make the layout real; nobody has measured them against the published profile. They are what @unvalidated is for, and settling them is exactly the work #595 describes — which is why the shape below matters more than the numbers do.

3bases

The app never says "popular" flatly

One record, three bases, three different sentences. This is the copy contract the client would be built against, and it is the part worth arguing over now — the wording is the feature.

BasisWhat the sheet saysWhat it actually claimsShips
named “We put this on a list of well-known stretches. That is an editorial call, not a measurement.” Somebody at OurHike thinks this is well known. Cheap to be wrong about, cheap to fix. v1 of the feature
published “The Appalachian Trail Conservancy lists this as a day hike.” ATC says so, and the claim is attributable to them rather than to us. when the source is registered
visited “25+ hikers using OurHike have sent a report, photo or thanks from here.” Where this app's users sent something — not where people hike. Biased to where the app has users, and it lags. empty on day one

No blended score, and no sort order that implies one. The three answer different questions. A leaderboard of trail sections is exactly what OurHikeValues.md #1 warns about — "anything that implies there's a 'right' way to hike" — wearing a map.

visited shows no count below k = 25, and then shows no line at all. Not "fewer than 25", which is itself a fact about a small group. Omit, never zero — the same restraint the shelter-capacity export already applies.

The club section does not follow the hiker up

#598 leaves this live: is a club section tappable above the seam too? The mockup says the polygon stops at the seam, and the sentence does not.

Above z9 the map is something a hiker is navigating by, and a thirty-run recolouring over it is clutter competing with pins that matter more. But "who maintains where I am standing" is a good question at any zoom — so it survives as one line at the foot of the legend, which is chrome the hiker opens deliberately, costs no map ink, and is already where this app puts its provenance sentences.

This is the cheap half of the answer, and it is reversible: if the line reads as clutter it is deleted without touching a layer, and if it reads as useful it can graduate to the status strip later.

5to approve

What I need decided before code

Four of these are questions the issues and features/CORRIDOR_VIEW.md already leave open. The fifth I found while reading the tree, and it is the one I would most like an answer to.

1. “Stretch” now means three different things in this repository.

cut_stretches.py (#552/#556, decided 2026-08-18 — after CORRIDOR_VIEW.md was written) uses stretch for a ~50-mile offline download unit. club_sections.json uses stretches for a club's contiguous runs of trail. #595 wants it for a named piece worth going to. Three records, one word, all live at once — and two of them would appear in the same sheet.

My recommendation The download unit keeps stretch; it is the newest decision, the most load-bearing, and already shipped in pipeline code. This feature's record becomes Highlight — a named piece of trail somebody says is worth going to. Renaming here costs one unshipped issue body; renaming cut_stretches.py costs a published artifact and a client half still to be built.

2. Thirty runs, drawn how?

Thirty distinct hues is illegible at z4.9, implies thirty categories that mean nothing to a hiker, and collides with blaze_color — which is how a trail line already gets its colour, and the AT is white-blazed end to end.

My recommendation Alternating tone of one hue, so adjacent clubs separate and nothing is ranked; a boundary tick at each seam; the selected run in blaze-orange; unattributed runs dashed and hollow. This also keeps blaze_color's meaning intact, which the alternative quietly spends.

3. How many named entries, and who owns the list?

#595's own words: "Twenty is a screen; two hundred is a gazetteer and a maintenance burden." An editorial list in a repository is a thing somebody has to answer for.

My recommendation Start at twelve, not twenty — one per state the trail crosses, minus the two it barely enters — each with a citation, in pipeline/reference/ where a reviewer reads it row by row. Twelve is small enough that being wrong about one is visible, and the list grows by pull request rather than by anybody's judgement at scale. This one is genuinely yours: it is editorial, and I should not pick the trail's landmarks on your behalf.

4. What does the corridor view do for a hiker who already has a fix?

#598 says cameraMemory.ts already decides this and the corridor view "should not re-decide it." Reading it: a fix moves no camera, deliberately — the camera is the hiker's from the first frame.

My recommendation Change nothing. No new camera behaviour, no auto-zoom to the hiker's club section. The mockups above show the same screens whether or not there is a fix. Confirming this is a decision, not an oversight.

5. Does visited count downloads?

CORRIDOR_VIEW.md leaves it open. A map package pulled from R2 is a server-side record, geographic by construction, and arguably a better signal of intent than a report — a hiker downloads a sheet before going, and reports only when something is wrong.

My recommendation No, and not now. visited ships empty either way, so this decides nothing on day one and can be taken later with real data in front of you. Counting downloads also turns a delivery log into a behavioural record, which is a bigger step than it looks and deserves its own issue rather than a line in this one.

What I would build, in what order

  1. Read the artifact. club_sections.json is published and no client file mentions it. A loader plus the manifest wiring, no UI — small, testable, lands alone.
  2. Draw the runs. The alternating-tone layer below POI_PIN_MIN_ZOOM, with the unattributed treatment. This is the App.tsx conflict surface #598 warns about — 12 of 27 recorded conflicts — so it goes in its own commit and stays narrow.
  3. The club sheet, on LineSheet.tsx's existing structure so the two read as one family, with the two source dates.
  4. The legend's maintainer line above the seam — decision 4's cheap half.
  5. Stop. Highlights need #595 to publish a record first, and that issue is unclaimed and free. The client half is a second branch, after.

What this deliberately is not

Not a trip planner. features/HIKE_PLANNING.md and features/SEGMENTS.md own planning. The moment this map holds a route somebody is building, it is that feature and belongs there.

Not cluster bubbles, and not the waypoint lanes. #532 asked for a choice between the two and CORRIDOR_VIEW.md refused both. Nothing in these mockups reopens that.
